What it does

Your iPad, full screen.
Your Mac, sharp.

Remote desktop apps stream whatever display your Mac has. Give it one the same shape as your iPad and the picture fills the glass, edge to edge.

Exact iPad sizes

Presets for every current iPad, down to the pixel. The 11" iPad Pro (M4) is 2420 × 1668, not the older 2388 × 1668 most tools ship.

Sharp by default

HiDPI at 2x, exactly how iPadOS draws. Want more room? Pick a larger "looks like" size from the same menu.

Mirror or extend

Mirror your MacBook screen, monitor or TV onto the iPad shape so there's one desktop, or make the virtual screen your main display. Portrait works too.

No more tiny text

Radish remembers the resolution you pick for each display and puts it back when the display reconnects or your Mac wakes.

Every resolution

A resolution picker for all your displays, including the HiDPI sizes System Settings hides.

Scriptable

radish://on, radish://off and radish://toggle work from Shortcuts, scripts or the address bar.

Built-in sizes

Every iPad, pixel for pixel.

Pick yours from New Virtual Screen. Anything else, enter a custom size.

Remote in from your iPad

Set up once with Jump Desktop.

Works the same with Screens, Parsec, Chrome Remote Desktop or anything else that streams your Mac's display.

Add your iPad

Click the radish in the menu bar, then New Virtual Screen and your iPad model. Say yes to Launch at Login.

Pick how it shows

Turn on Mirror Other Displays. Your MacBook screen or monitor shows the same desktop, and Jump streams it in your iPad's shape.

Connect

In Jump Desktop's settings on the iPad, turn on Retina resolution, then connect. No black bars, nothing tiny.

For a Mac you reach from anywhere

  • Keep it awake: turn on "Prevent automatic sleeping when the display is off" in Energy settings, or use an app like Amphetamine.
  • Turn on "Start up automatically after a power failure" so it comes back by itself.
  • Turn on Connect screens when Radish opens in General so your screen comes back after a restart too.
  • With FileVault on, nothing runs after a restart until someone logs in at the Mac. That's macOS, not Radish.
Questions

Good to know

Why does Radish need to keep running?

macOS removes a virtual screen when the app that made it quits. Launch at Login keeps Radish in the menu bar, so your screen is one click away. To have it on every time, turn on Connect screens when Radish opens in General.

Does it need any permissions?

No. Radish asks for nothing, makes no network requests and collects nothing. It uses the same macOS display API as other virtual display tools.

Where did my window go?

An extended virtual screen is a real display you can't see on the Mac itself. Turn on Mirror Other Displays, or switch the screen off from the menu and macOS moves its windows back.

Something looks wrong. Where do I look?

Radish logs what it does to ~/Library/Logs/Radish.log. Include it when you report a problem.
